\begin{abstract}
We study an interacting spinless Lieb-Kitaev chain in the nearly flat-band regime, motivated by the recent observation that Majorana zero modes in the non-interacting Lieb-Kitaev system inherit two decay modes: an ordinary Bogoliubov-de Gennes localization length and a longer geometric length controlled by the quantum metric . Using finite-system density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) calculations , we analyze how these structures evolve under repulsive nearest-neighbor density interaction. At weak coupling the data remain compatible with a surviving long geometric Majorana scale, whereas at larger repulsion the system exhibits charge order, showing repeated transitions between commensurate and incommensurate CDW phases, and a conventional density-ordered phase in the strong coupling regime. These results support the view that in flat-band topological superconductors quantum geometry and interaction-driven ordering are deeply intertwined.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Over the past two decades, flat-band materials have attracted considerable interest. Owing to the quenching of kinetic energy in flat bands, interaction effects are substantially enhanced and can dominate the low-energy physics, rendering flat-band systems a fertile platform for investigating strongly correlated phenomena and interaction-driven quantum phases. This has led to the realization of a wide range of exotic states, including unconventional superconductivity~\cite{caoUnconventionalSuperconductivityMagicangle2018,peottaSuperfluidityTopologicallyNontrivial2015a,yankowitzTuningSuperconductivityTwisted2019},  magnetism ~\cite{repellinFerromagnetismNarrowBands2020,linFlatbandsEmergentFerromagnetic2018}, and fractional topological phases ~\cite{tangHighTemperatureFractionalQuantum2011,regnaultFractionalChernInsulator2011,royBandGeometryFractional2014}. 

For a superconducting (SC) phase within a conventional single-band BCS picture, a perfectly flat band has vanishing band curvature and thus an infinite effective mass would appear to strongly inhibit superconductivity. In contrast, in multiband flat-band systems, the complete quenching of the band dispersion does not eliminate the nontrivial momentum-space structure of the Bloch wave functions~\cite{huhtinenRevisitingFlatBand2022,tormaQuantumMetricEffective2018a,huGeometricConventionalContributions2025,tovmasyanEffectiveTheoryEmergent2016a}. The Bloch states can retain a finite extent in Hilbert space across the Brillouin zone, reflecting their variation with crystal momentum, and this quadratic spread is quantified by the quantum metric through the infinitesimal distance between neighboring Bloch states~\cite{restaInsulatingStateMatter2011,chengQuantumGeometricTensor2010,yuQuantumGeometryQuantum2025}. Such nontrivial quantum geometry provides an additional channel for superconducting transport that is absent in the conventional single-band picture. As a consequence, a finite superfluid weight and robust superconducting coherence can persist even in the flat-band limit, despite the absence of conventional band-dispersion contributions~\cite{peottaPeottaHeTorma2015Superfluidity2015,xieTopologyBoundedSuperfluidWeight2020a,tormaSuperconductivitySuperfluidityQuantum2022a,julkuGeometricOriginSuperfluidity2016a}.


The superconducting coherence length is a fundamental length scale that characterizes the spatial extent of superconducting correlations. In conventional BCS superconductors, it is given by \(\xi_{\mathrm{BCS}}\sim \hbar v_F/\Delta\), with \(v_F\) the kinetic energy  and  \(\Delta\) the pairing gap . This length scale measures the characteristic size of Cooper pairs. By comparison, recent developments have shown that the quantum metric of Bloch wave functions not only generates a finite geometric contribution to the superfluid weight, but also establishes an intrinsic length scale governing superconducting phenomena~\cite{chenGinzburgLandauTheoryFlat2024,huAnomalousCoherenceLength2025,yangQuantumGeometricKondo2026}. This geometric viewpoint became significant in the recent analysis of Majorana zero modes in the noninteracting Lieb-Kitaev model ~\cite{guoMajoranaZeroModes2025}. There, the edge mode does not exhibit a single localization length. Instead, one finds two distinct correlation lengths: a short Bogoliubov-de Gennes scale associated with ordinary topological superconducting localization ~\cite{kitaevUnpairedMajoranaFermions2001,miaoMajoranaZeroModes2018}, and a longer geometric scale inherited from the spread of flat-band wave functions. The latter is controlled by the quantum metric and can become parametrically large in the nearly flat-band regime. This immediately raises a  many-body question: once repulsive interactions are introduced, does the geometric long tail survive as a physically meaningful scale, or is it washed out by correlation-driven reconstruction?

In previous studies, interactions were typically introduced in the form of an attractive pairing channel, which directly promotes Cooper pairing and thereby induces superconductivity.
Representative examples in one dimension include stub~\cite{thuminFlatBandSuperconductivity2023}, sawtooth~\cite{chanPairingSuperconductivityQuasi2022}, Creutz~\cite{chanPairingSuperconductivityQuasi2022,mondainiPairingSuperconductivityFlat2018,tovmasyanEffectiveTheoryEmergent2016a,salernoDrudeWeightManybody2023}, and related multiorbital chains. Here, we retain the attractive interaction responsible for superconducting pairing while introducing a repulsive interaction to incorporate competing correlation effects beyond the pairing channel. Such a coexistence of attractive and repulsive interactions is physically natural in many electronic systems: an effective attractive pairing interaction may arise from phonons, collective fluctuations, or superexchange processes, while residual Coulomb interactions remain repulsive. This situation is encountered, for example, in electron-phonon superconductors~\cite{morelCalculationSuperconductingState1962,sacepeQuantumBreakdownSuperconductivity2020}, moiré flat-band systems~\cite{wuTheoryPhononMediatedSuperconductivity2018,lianTwistedBilayerGraphene2019}, and extended Hubbard models~\cite{pencPhaseDiagramOnedimensional1994,aligiaPhaseDiagramOnedimensional2000}, where the competition between pairing and repulsion can substantially reshape the superconducting state and stabilize competing charge-ordered phases.


To address this question beyond mean-field theory, we employ density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) calculations to investigate the interacting one-dimensional flat-band superconductor. By systematically resolving its phase structure, correlation functions, and characteristic length scales, we aim to determine how repulsive interactions reshape superconductivity and whether the quantum-geometric length scale remains robust in the many-body regime.


\section{Model and theoretical framework}
\label{sec:model}

We consider an interacting Lieb-Kiatev chain model, governed by the Hamiltonian  

\begin{equation}
H = H_0 + H_U,
\end{equation}

with

\begin{align}
H_0
={}&-t\sum_j\sum_{\alpha=A,B,C}
\left(
c_{j\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{j+1,\alpha}
+\mathrm{h.c.}
\right)
\nonumber\\
&-V\sum_j c_{jA}^{\dagger}c_{jA}
+V\sum_j c_{jC}^{\dagger}c_{jC}
\nonumber\\
&-J\sum_j
\left(
c_{jA}^{\dagger}c_{jB}
+c_{jC}^{\dagger}c_{jB}
+\mathrm{h.c.}
\right)
\nonumber\\
&-J\sum_j
\left(
c_{jA}^{\dagger}c_{j+1,B}
+c_{jC}^{\dagger}c_{j+1,B}
+\mathrm{h.c.}
\right)
\nonumber\\
&+\Delta\sum_j\sum_{\alpha=A,B,C}
\left(
c_{j\alpha}c_{j+1,\alpha}
+\mathrm{h.c.}
\right).
\end{align}

and 

\begin{equation}
H_U = U \sum_j\sum_{\alpha=A,B,C} \hat{n}_{j\alpha} \hat{n}_{j+1,\alpha}
\end{equation}

The Lieb–Kitaev model describes a one-dimensional three-orbital lattice at open boundary condition with orbitals \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) in each unit cell $j$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:1}(a)) . The onsite energies of the three orbitals are shifted by \(-V\), \(0\), and \(+V\). The hopping amplitude \(J\) couples the \(B\) orbital to the neighboring \(A\) and \(C\) orbitals, both within the same unit cell and between adjacent unit cells, forming the characteristic Lieb-like lattice geometry.  To realize a topological superconducting phase, nearest-neighbor intra-orbital pairing with amplitude \(\Delta\) is introduced between the same orbitals in adjacent unit cells. In addition, a weaker hopping \(t\) connects identical orbitals in neighboring unit cells and gives the otherwise flat middle band a small dispersion: $\epsilon_0= -2t \cos(ka)$ ,where $a$ is the lattice constant.

To maintain the flat-band regime, the dispersion of the middle band, characterized by the energy scale \(2t\), should be much smaller than the separation from the dispersive bands, \(E_g=|V|\) and the dominant hopping $J$. When we choose parameters  $t=0.03 , V=0.6 , J=1 , \Delta=0.018$, the flat-band condition remains satisfied (Fig.~\ref{fig:1}(b)).


Recent work ~\cite{guoMajoranaZeroModes2025} showed that the Majorana edge profile in the Lieb-Kitaev model inherits both a short localization length and a longer scale tied to $\xi_{\rm QM}$. For the noninteracting parent model, the bulk property given by Bloch states $\lvert u_n(k)\rangle$ define the quantum geometric tensor

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Q}_{\mu\nu}(k)
=
\langle \partial_\mu u(k)\vert
\left[1-\lvert u(k)\rangle\langle u(k)\rvert\right]
\vert \partial_\nu u(k)\rangle,
\end{equation}


Solving the hamiltonian in momentum space, the eigenstate of the middle band can
be written as
\begin{equation}
    |u_0(k)\rangle
    =
    \frac{1}
    {\sqrt{4J^2[1+\cos(ka)]+V^2}}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        a_k \\
        V \\
        -a_k
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

where  $a_k=-J(1+e^{ika})$ . We define the quantum metric length through the Brillouin-zone-averaged quantum metric

\begin{equation}
    \xi_{\rm QM}
    \equiv
    \frac{1}{a}
    \frac{a}{2\pi}
    \int_{\rm BZ} dk\,\mathcal{Q}_{\mu\nu}(k)
    \simeq
    \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}
    \frac{J}{V}a
\end{equation}

On the other hand, the Majorana wave function under open boundary conditions can be
constructed from the projected bulk Green's function,
\begin{equation}
    \psi_L(x)
    \propto
    G^p(x,x_{\rm L};0)\chi_{\rm L},
\end{equation}

where

\begin{equation}
    G^p(x,x';0)
    =
    \sum_{n=\pm}
    \int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,
    \frac{g_n(k)g_n^\dagger(k)}
    {-\epsilon_n(k)}
    e^{ik(x-x')}.
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_n(k)$ denotes the dispersion of the two low-energy
Bogoliubov quasiparticle bands, while $g_n(k)$ is the corresponding
BdG Bloch eigenstate.  The vector $\chi_{\rm L}$
encodes the local boundary coupling that generates the left Majorana
mode under open boundary conditions.

The low-energy BdG eigenstates can be factorized as
\begin{equation}
    g_{\pm}(k)
    =
    v_{\pm}(k)\otimes u_0(k),
\end{equation}

Here, $v_{\pm}(k)$ describes the particle--hole structure of the
effective Kitaev sector, whereas $u_0(k)$ is the Bloch eigenstate of
the normal-state flat band and describes its orbital structure.
Accordingly, the projected Green's function contains two distinct
momentum-dependent factors. The quasiparticle part, consisting of
$v_{\pm}(k)v_{\pm}^{\dagger}(k)$ together with
$1/\epsilon_{\pm}(k)$, produces poles associated with the BdG
quasiparticle dispersion and gives the conventional localization
length $\xi_{\rm BCS}$. In contrast, the orbital projector
$u_0(k)u_0^{\dagger}(k)$ contains additional poles originating from
the momentum dependence of the flat-band Bloch wave function. These
poles generate the geometric localization length that is just $4\xi_{\rm QM} =\frac{\sqrt{2} J}{V}a $.
Consequently, the Majorana wave function contains two exponentially
decaying components,
\begin{equation}
    \psi_{\rm L}(x)
    =
    A_{\rm BCS}e^{-x/\xi_{\rm BCS}}
    +
    A_{\rm QM}e^{-x/(4\xi_{\rm QM})}.
\end{equation}


\section{Numerical strategy}
\label{sec:numerics}

The interacting ground state is computed by finite-system DMRG with open boundary conditions using ITensor~\cite{fishman2022}. The three-orbital chain is mapped to a one-dimensional matrix-product-state sequence $(A_1,B_1,C_1,A_2,B_2,C_2,\dots,A_L,B_L,C_L)$. We choose the parameter $t=0.03$, $V=0.6$, $J=1$ and $\Delta=0.018$ and restrict the system to half filling for this spinless model. The source calculations emphasize careful sweep scheduling and convergence control, which is essential because the entanglement rises substantially in parts of the repulsive regime and because flat-band systems generally exhibit a large degree of degeneracy.

The nontrivial decay modes in this model can be characterized by the superconducting coherence length, which is conventionally extracted from the pair correlation function and remains applicable in flat-band systems. Here we adopt an alternative approach based on the geometric character of the flat band. In this case, the relevant superconducting length scale is related to the quadratic spread of the Wannier states, which is governed by the bloch functions and can be directly accessed through single-particle correlation functions $\langle c^{\dagger}_i c_j\rangle$ , $\langle c_i c_j\rangle$. These quantities can be used to characterize the long-range collective decay modes, and after extrapolation to the thermodynamic limit, the extracted values are consistent with the theoretical predictions.

We use central charge to justify an ICDW phase . In one dimension, the ICDW phase is typically gapless and is described by a Luttinger liquid, so the entanglement entropy follows the conformal scaling form with a finite central charge, usually \(c=1\) for a single gapless charge mode. By contrast, a commensurate CDW phase is generally gapped, for which the entanglement entropy saturates at long distances and the effective central charge vanishes, \(c=0\). A fully gapped one-dimensional superconducting phase, including a gapped topological superconducting phase, has no gapless bulk degrees of freedom and therefore corresponds to an effective central charge \(c=0\). Central charge can be obtained from entanglement entropy with the Calabrese–Cardy formula at open boundary condition~\cite{pasqualecalabreseEntanglementEntropyQuantum2004}.


\begin{equation}
S(\ell)=\frac{c}{6}\ln\left[\frac{2L}{\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi \ell}{L}\right)\right]+s_0.
\end{equation}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}



Our DMRG calculations reveal a rich interaction-driven phase diagram in the half-filled interacting Lieb--Kitaev chain. Starting from weak interactions, the system first remains in the gapped topological superconducting phase continuously connected to the noninteracting Lieb--Kitaev limit, where the quantum-metric-induced length scale remains visible in the long-distance single-particle correlations. Upon increasing the nearest-neighbor repulsion \(U\), the system enters an incommensurate charge-density-wave (ICDW) regime for approximately \(0.1\lesssim U\lesssim0.3\), characterized by spatially modulated charge correlations and a finite central charge \(c\simeq1\), indicating a gapless Luttinger-liquid-like mode. This is followed by a reentrant gapped topological regime for \(0.3\lesssim U\lesssim0.8\), with a vanishing central charge and saturated entanglement entropy. At stronger interactions, another gapless ICDW regime emerges for \(0.8\lesssim U\lesssim2.5\), again accompanied by \(c\simeq1\), before the system finally evolves into a strongly coupled commensurate CDW phase for \(U\gtrsim2.5\), where the charge density becomes locked into a period-two pattern and the central charge returns to \(c=0\). In the intermediate regimes, the charge modulations are accompanied by corresponding bond- and pair-density modulations, reflecting the strongly intertwined charge, bond, and superconducting correlations of the multiorbital flat-band system.

This evolution is most directly visualized through
$n_\alpha(i)$. At weak coupling, the density is spatially
uniform within each orbital, although the average
occupations of different orbitals need not coincide.
Incommensurate modulations subsequently develop on all
three orbitals, producing an $ABC$-ICDW pattern.
With increasing interaction, the $A$ and $C$ densities
lock into pronounced period-two CDW configurations,
while the $B$ density becomes nearly uniform again.
The next regime retains this commensurate $AC$
background, but the $B$ orbital develops a renewed
incommensurate density response. Finally, the $B$
density also locks into a period-two pattern as its
average occupation approaches half filling, completing
the formation of an $ABC$-CDW state. The $B$ orbital
thus undergoes a distinctive sequence of modulation,
flattening, and renewed modulation before joining
the final commensurate state.


\subsection{Phase I: topological phase}

The weak-interaction regime labelled TC ($0\leq U\lesssim0.1$)
is characterized by spatially uniform bulk observables and a
noncritical entanglement structure.
(Fig~\ref{fig:TC_summary}(a)--(c)) show representative results at
$U=0.05$ for $N=240$.
The normal bond expectation values
$\langle c^\dagger_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$ and pairing
expectation values $\langle c_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$
likewise approach uniform bulk values for $\alpha=A,B,C$.
Thus, no bulk CDW, intraorbital BOW, or PDW modulation is resolved
in these profiles. The nonzero, spatially uniform pairing amplitudes
are compatible with the explicit pairing term in the Hamiltonian.

The entanglement entropy reaches an essentially size-independent
plateau for $N=120$ and $240$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:TC_summary}(d)]).
Fitting the central half of the chain yields $c_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq0$, consistent with a noncritical bulk.
The normal correlation function on the $B$ orbital,
\begin{equation}
 G_B(r)=\langle c^\dagger_{iB}c_{i+r,B}\rangle,
 \qquad i=40,
\end{equation}
exhibits approximately exponential decay
(Fig.~\ref{fig:TC_summary}(e)) with $\xi\simeq2.375,2.438,2.534,2.632$ unit cells at
$U=0,0.02,0.05,0.08$, respectively.
The fitted decay length increases with interaction strength,
while the approximately exponential form persists over the
examined interval. Deviations from a single exponential become
more visible at $U=0.08$.




We therefore identify phase I as the topological reference state from which the interaction-driven reconstruction begins.  The correlation function exhibits the smooth long-ranged structure expected for the topological parent state, while the entropy profile remains consistent with a gapped regime. The sublattice occupations are already unequal, but they do not yet show the pronounced interaction-driven incommensurate locking that appears at larger $U$.

\subsection{Phase II: ICDW phase }

The second regime develops simultaneous modulations of the density,
bond expectation values, and pairing amplitudes.
At $U=0.24$, the orbital-resolved profiles in
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase2}(a)--(c)) display spatial oscillations with a
slowly varying envelope, in contrast to the uniform bulk profiles
of the TC regime. Corresponding oscillations occur in both
$\langle c^\dagger_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$ and
$\langle c_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$ for
$\alpha=A,B,C$, identifying coupled density, bond, and pairing
modulations on the finite chains. Moreover, the dominant
Fourier component of each mean-subtracted bulk profile occurs
at the same resolved wave vector, $Q\simeq0.95\pi$, with the
unit-cell spacing set to unity. This common component,
displaced from the period-two value $Q=\pi$, supports an
$ABC$ charge modulation with incommensurate character on the
studied finite chain.

For $N=240$, the entanglement entropy exhibits
oscillations about a smooth profile (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase2}(d)).
We estimate the effective central charge.A fit over $L/4\leq\ell\leq3L/4$ yields $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.021$ for $N=240$.
These estimates are compatible with a $c\simeq1$ critical
regime.

The mean occupation of the $B$ orbital provides a complementary
measure of the interaction-driven density redistribution.
Defining a bulk average over the central half of the chain,$\overline{n}_B=\frac{2}{L}
\sum_{i=L/4+1}^{3L/4}\langle c^\dagger_{iB}c_{iB}\rangle$we plot its 9evolution over the interval$0\leq U\leq3$, for $N=120$ and $240$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase2}(e)).

One may relate the incommensurate phase to the doped Luttinger liquid inspired by the Kitaev-Hubbard model~\cite{mahyaehStudyPhaseDiagram2020,rahmaniPhaseDiagramInteracting2015,stoudenmireInteractionEffectsTopological2011a}. Even if the system is a nearly flat band, it still perserves a small dispersion. It therefore permits a low-energy expansion into right-
and left-moving modes, providing the kinetic basis for a
Luttinger-liquid description. The occupation of the $B$ orbital at $U=0.24$ is
$\overline{n}_B=0.43859$ corresponds to a doped orbital
$\delta_B\equiv1/2-\overline{n}_B\simeq0.06141$
relative to half filling. Together with the estimate
$c_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq1$, this motivates a phenomenological
interpretation in terms of a doped Luttinger liquid.
In a single-component spinless Luttinger description, the
low-energy excitations are collective density fluctuations,
and the oscillatory part of the bulk connected density
correlation has the form ~\cite{Voit1995}
\begin{equation}
 C_B^{\mathrm{osc}}(r)\sim
 A_B\frac{\cos(Qr)}{r^{2K}},
 \qquad Q=2k_F^{\mathrm{eff}},
 \label{eq:phase2_icdw_ll}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the Luttinger parameter and the unit-cell spacing
is set to unity. Doping the effective low-energy band away
from a commensurate filling shifts $Q$ away from its
commensurate value and introduces a spatial mismatch in the
corresponding umklapp term. When the density field remains
unlocked, this mismatch suppresses commensurate pinning at
long distances, allowing an incommensurate density response
in a gapless liquid~\cite{Giamarchi1997}.

\subsection{Phase III: intermediate CDW phase }

The third regime is represented by $U=0.50$ and $N=240$.
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase3}(a)) shows a pronounced period-two density
pattern on the $A$ and $C$ orbitals, while the $B$-orbital
occupation remains nearly uniform in the bulk.
To quantify this distinction, we define the staggered component
of a local observable $O_\alpha(i)$ by
\begin{equation}
 m_{O,\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}
 \left|\overline{O}_{\alpha,\mathrm{even}}
       -\overline{O}_{\alpha,\mathrm{odd}}\right|,
 \label{eq:phase3_staggering}
\end{equation}
where the averages are taken separately over even and odd
unit cells in the central half of the chain.
The density amplitudes are $m_{n,A}=0.2125$ and
$m_{n,C}=0.1964$, whereas $m_{n,B}\simeq1.9\times10^{-6}$.
The two large amplitudes vary by less than $0.2\%$ between
$N=120$ and $240$. These results support a commensurate
$AC$-CDW pattern, with no comparable density staggering
resolved on the $B$ orbital.

The bond and pairing profiles reveal a complementary orbital
structure (Figs.~\ref{fig:phase3}(b) and (c)).
For $B_\alpha(i)=\langle c^\dagger_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$
and $P_\alpha(i)=\langle c_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$,
we obtain $m_{B,B}=7.45\times10^{-4}$ and
$m_{P,B}=6.36\times10^{-4}$.
The corresponding staggered components on $A$ and $C$ are
below $10^{-6}$. Thus, the $B$ orbital supports alternating
bond and pairing amplitudes despite its nearly uniform
local density. The pairing modulation coexists with a
nonzero uniform component, $\overline{P}_B=-0.00455$. The entanglement entropy approaches two nearly constant
branches associated with even and odd cuts
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase3}(d)). Taken together, the profiles support a third regime with
commensurate $AC$-CDW and $B$-orbital BOW/PDW modulations.

\subsection{Phase IV: second ICDW phase}

The fourth regime is represented by $U=1.50$ and $N=240$.
The density profiles in (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase4}(a)) retain pronounced
period-two components on the $A$ and $C$ orbitals.
Defining the bulk staggered amplitude as
$m_{O,\alpha}=|\overline{O}_{\alpha,\mathrm{even}}
-\overline{O}_{\alpha,\mathrm{odd}}|/2$, , we find $m_{n,A}=0.2875$ and
$m_{n,C}=0.2970$. The strong density staggering thus
remains concentrated on $A$ and $C$, while the $B$ channel
develops a distinct, weaker density response.

The bond and pair expectation values show that the $B$-orbital
modulations persist (Figs.~\ref{fig:phase4}(b) and (c)).
For $B_\alpha(i)=\langle c^\dagger_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$
and $P_\alpha(i)=\langle c_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$,
the staggered amplitudes are $m_{B,B}=5.88\times10^{-2}$
and $m_{P,B}=8.41\times10^{-5}$.
Their counterparts on $A$ and $C$ are at most
$1.8\times10^{-4}$ and $1.4\times10^{-6}$, respectively.
These data support predominantly $B$-orbital BOW and PDW
modulations coexisting with the $AC$-CDW background.

The decisive distinction from the third regime is the recovery
of logarithmic entanglement scaling (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase4}(d)),
with a fit over $20\leq\ell\leq60$ yielding $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.037$ for $N=240$. In this regime, the spatially modulated $B$-orbital density
is interpreted as an incommensurate charge-density-wave
(ICDW) response, coexisting with commensurate CDW order
on the $A$ and $C$ orbitals.  As shown above (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase2}(e)), increasing $U$ drives the average $B$-orbital
occupation from a doped value toward half filling.
Together with the evolution of the spatial profiles, this
trend may suggest a sequence of commensurate--incommensurate
(C--IC) transitions, reflecting competition between
commensurate locking and a density modulation whose preferred
wave vector evolves with the orbital occupation.
Upon approaching half filling, commensurate locking becomes
favored, and the $B$-orbital density develops a period-two
CDW pattern. This interpretation relies on the density
modulations as well as the occupation: the approach of
$\overline{n}_B$ to $1/2$ alone does not establish the
intermediate C--IC transitions.



\subsection{Phase V: strong-coupling CDW phase}

The fifth regime is represented by $U=5.0$ and $N=240$.
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase5}(a)) shows pronounced period-two density
modulations on all three orbitals. The bulk staggered amplitudes are $m_{n,A}=0.4875$,
$m_{n,B}=0.4770$, and $m_{n,C}=0.4876$, where
$m_{O,\alpha}=|\overline{O}_{\alpha,\mathrm{even}}
-\overline{O}_{\alpha,\mathrm{odd}}|/2$ is evaluated over
$21\leq i\leq60$.
The $B$ orbital is nearly half filled,
$\overline{n}_B=0.500006$, with occupations alternating between
approximately $0.9770$ and $0.0230$.
Its approach to half filling is thus accompanied by strong
commensurate density ordering. The entanglement entropy is correspondingly small,
saturating at approximately $0.062$ and $0.080$ for odd
and even bulk cuts, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase5}(c)).
This weak entanglement is consistent with a state close
to a classical charge configuration and, together with
the pronounced density staggering. These results support an $ABC$-CDW
phase.

The bond profiles reveal a predominantly $AC$-BOW component
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase5}(b)). For
$B_\alpha(i)=\langle c^\dagger_{i\alpha}c_{i+1,\alpha}\rangle$,
the staggered amplitudes on $A$ and $C$ are both approximately
$2.12\times10^{-2}$, whereas the $B$-orbital amplitude is only
$2.23\times10^{-6}$.
The dominant bond modulation therefore shifts from $B$ in
the fourth regime to $A$ and $C$ in the fifth regime.
The pair expectation values also retain weak spatial
modulations, with amplitudes
$9.77\times10^{-7}$ and $1.30\times10^{-6}$ on $A$ and $C$.
In this strong-coupling regime, the large intercell
repulsion favors nearly classical charge configurations
and suppresses pair fluctuations, making an independent
PDW instability less likely.
We therefore characterize this regime as an $ABC$-CDW state
with predominantly $AC$ bond modulation, without assigning
an additional PDW order.





\section{Conclusion and Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion}


We have investigated the interaction-driven evolution of
the Lieb--Kitaev chain using orbital-resolved densities,
bond and pairing expectation values, and entanglement
entropy. The resulting five regimes reveal how quantum
geometry, orbital charge redistribution, and commensurate
ordering shape the physics of an interacting nearly
flat-band system. Their distinction requires resolving
the individual orbitals, whose density and bond responses
can evolve differently under the same interaction.

In the first regime, the bulk density remains spatially
uniform within each orbital, and the entanglement entropy
saturates with a negligible logarithmic contribution.
The single-particle correlations decay exponentially,
with a fitted decay length close to $4\xi_{\mathrm{QM}}$
near the noninteracting limit, where $\xi_{\mathrm{QM}}$
is estimated from the underlying band geometry.
This numerical agreement supports a quantum-geometric
interpretation of the correlation length in the
weakly interacting regime and connects the real-space
correlations to the geometry of the nearly flat band.

Increasing the interaction produces a sequence of
orbital-selective density reorganizations, accompanied
by changes in entanglement scaling and bond modulation.
Incommensurate density modulations first develop on all
three orbitals in a critical regime compatible with
$c_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq1$. The $A$ and $C$ densities
subsequently lock into period-two CDW patterns while
the $B$ density becomes nearly uniform, accompanied
by entropy saturation and predominantly $B$-orbital
bond modulation. A renewed incommensurate response
then appears on $B$ within the commensurate $AC$
background, together with the recovery of logarithmic
entanglement scaling. Finally, as the average $B$
occupation approaches half filling, its density also
locks into a period-two pattern, completing the
$ABC$-CDW state. The small, saturated entropy in this
strong-coupling regime supports a nearly classical
charge configuration, while the dominant bond
modulation shifts to $A$ and $C$. The residual pairing
modulation remains weak and does not establish an
independent PDW phase in the presence of explicit
pairing. Together, these observations suggest a
competition between commensurate locking and
incommensurate density responses, with distinct
orbitals participating at different interaction
strengths.

Future work should establish the thermodynamic
transition boundaries through larger-system calculations,
wave-vector scaling, and excitation-gap analysis.
Pair-correlation and pairing-field studies could
clarify the origin of the weak pairing response.
Varying the residual bandwidth and orbital
hybridization would further test how quantum geometry
controls the correlation length and the subsequent
sequence of orbital-selective ordering.
