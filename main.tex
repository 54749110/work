\title{Interaction-driven multiphase structure in an interacting flat-band Lieb-Kitaev chain}

\author{Jiale Xuan}
\affiliation{School of Physics and Astronomy, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai 200240, China}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We revisit the interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain in the nearly flat-band regime and reorganize the numerical evidence into a five-phase picture. The analysis is based directly on DMRG outputs for entanglement entropy, orbital-resolved occupation profiles, Majorana-related correlation functions, and bond observables resolved in real space. Instead of emphasizing only scans versus interaction strength, we examine representative points inside each phase and use their spatial patterns as the primary evidence. The resulting interpretation supports the sequence of a weak-coupling topological phase, an incommensurate charge-density-wave phase, an intermediate phase with coexisting topological and density-ordered structures, a second incommensurate regime, and finally a strong-coupling charge-density-wave phase. The central physical message is that the repulsive interaction does not simply destroy topological superconductivity. Rather, it redistributes the orbital content and reconstructs the low-energy state differently on the $A$, $B$, and $C$ sublattices, producing distinct combinations of topological, incommensurate, bond-ordered, and pair-density-wave-like behavior in different interaction windows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Flat-band superconductivity provides a natural setting in which quantum geometry and many-body interaction effects become inseparable~\cite{peotta2015,julku2016,torma2022}. In ordinary dispersive superconductors, the dominant length scales are controlled primarily by band curvature and pairing amplitude. In contrast, in multiorbital flat-band systems the internal structure of Bloch wave functions itself contributes to physical observables, including superfluid response and localization properties. The Lieb geometry is especially attractive in this context because it supports nearly flat bands together with strongly structured orbital weight.

Recent work on the noninteracting Lieb-Kitaev model sharpened this perspective by showing that the Majorana zero mode need not be characterized by a single spatial scale~\cite{chen2024,guo2025}. Instead, two lengths emerge: a short Bogoliubov-de Gennes localization length and a longer geometric length associated with the quantum metric of the parent band. This immediately suggests a many-body problem of broader interest. Once interactions are introduced, does the topological state survive in a recognizable form, and if so, how is the geometric structure reconstructed?

The question is nontrivial because interactions are most important precisely when the band becomes flat. Reduced kinetic energy enhances charge redistribution and competing order. In one-dimensional topological superconductors, repulsive interactions already generate rich competition among topology, charge order, and criticality~\cite{katsura2015,gangadharaiah2011,mahyaeh2020}. In the multiorbital Lieb-Kitaev chain, that competition is further enriched by orbital selectivity and by the possibility that different sublattices respond differently to the same interaction.

This project also has a broader conceptual motivation summarized in our working notes~\cite{flatbandnotes}. On the attractive side, a negative-$U$ interaction can induce superconductivity even without inserting an explicit pairing field $\Delta$, which highlights the intrinsic superconducting tendency of the flat-band system. On the repulsive side, once a finite $\Delta$ is present, positive $U$ does not merely suppress the paired state uniformly; instead it opens a structured phase diagram in which the sublattices may realize different kinds of density, bond, and pairing textures. The present manuscript focuses on this repulsive branch and formulates the current numerical evidence in a PRB-style research format.

\section{Model and numerical framework}
\label{sec:model}

We study a spinless interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain with three orbitals $(A,B,C)$ per unit cell. The microscopic Hamiltonian is written as
\begin{equation}
H = H_t + H_\Delta + H_V + H_U,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
H_t &= \sum_j \Big[
t_1 a_j^\dagger b_j + t_1 c_j^\dagger b_j
+ t_2 a_{j+1}^\dagger b_j + t_2 c_j^\dagger b_{j+1}
+ \text{H.c.}
\Big], \\
H_\Delta &= \sum_j \Big[
\Delta_1 a_j b_j + \Delta_1 c_j b_j
+ \Delta_2 a_{j+1} b_j + \Delta_2 c_j b_{j+1}
+ \text{H.c.}
\Big], \\
H_V &= V \sum_j (\hat n_{C,j}-\hat n_{A,j})
- \mu \sum_j (\hat n_{A,j}+\hat n_{B,j}+\hat n_{C,j}), \\
H_U &= U \sum_j \left(\hat n_j-\frac12\right)\left(\hat n_{j+1}-\frac12\right).
\end{align}
The representative parameter set used throughout the present dataset is
\begin{equation}
t=0.03,\qquad J=1,\qquad V=0.6,\qquad \Delta=0.018,\qquad \mu=0.
\end{equation}

The interacting ground state is computed by finite-system DMRG with open boundary conditions using ITensor~\cite{fishman2022}. Rather than relying only on phase-boundary indicators obtained from scans in $U$, we analyze representative real-space observables inside each phase: the entanglement entropy $S(\ell)$, the orbital-resolved density profile $n_\alpha(x)$, a Majorana-related correlation function in the bulk, and nearest-distance normal correlators used here as bond-order diagnostics. In the discussion below, the $A$-sector bond indicator is represented by the real-space pattern of $\langle A^\dagger B\rangle$ [labeled as $\mathrm{AdagB}$ in the data files], while the $B$-sector bond indicator is represented by $\langle B^\dagger B\rangle$ [labeled as $\mathrm{BdagB}$].

\section{Results: five phases}
\label{sec:results}

The numerical evidence is most naturally organized into five regimes. We denote them as phase I (topological), phase II (ICDW), phase III (intermediate), phase IV (second ICDW), and phase V (strong-coupling CDW). The discussion below follows this classification and uses representative values of $U$ chosen from inside each phase.

\subsection{Phase I: topological phase}

Phase I is identified as the weak-coupling topological regime. Figure~\ref{fig:phase1} shows three representative observables at $U=0$: a Majorana-related correlation function, the entanglement entropy, and the orbital-resolved density profile. The correlation function is smooth and long-ranged in the sense expected for the topological parent state, and the entropy profile is consistent with a gapped phase rather than a strongly modulated critical state. At the same time, the orbital density pattern shows that the three sublattices are already inequivalent even before interaction-driven density ordering becomes important.

\begin{figure*}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase1_U0_corr.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase1_U0_EE.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase1_U0_ni.png}
  \caption{Phase I (topological phase) represented by $U=0$. Left: Majorana-related correlation function. Middle: entanglement entropy profile. Right: orbital-resolved occupation profile. Together these observables support a weak-coupling topological phase with sublattice asymmetry but without incommensurate density locking.}
  \label{fig:phase1}
\end{figure*}

This phase is therefore the interacting continuation of the Lieb-Kitaev topological superconductor in the limit where repulsion has not yet reconstructed the real-space order pattern. In our interpretation, phase I is topological rather than density ordered.

\subsection{Phase II: ICDW phase at $U=0.2$}

At $U=0.2$ the spatial structure changes qualitatively. Figure~\ref{fig:phase2} shows the entropy profile, the orbital-resolved densities, and the bond observables on the $A$ and $B$ sectors. The density plot now displays incommensurate modulation on all three sublattices, which motivates the classification of the whole system as an $ABC$ incommensurate charge-density-wave (ICDW) state. In addition, the $A$-sector bond observable develops a pronounced oscillatory pattern, which we interpret as an incommensurate bond-order-wave (IBOW) feature on the $A$ orbital.

\begin{figure*}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase2_U0p2_EE.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase2_U0p2_ni.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase2_U0p2_A_bond.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase2_U0p2_B_bond.png}
  \caption{Phase II (ICDW phase) represented by $U=0.2$. Top left: entanglement entropy. Top right: orbital-resolved occupation profile. Bottom left: $A$-sector bond observable represented by $\langle A^\dagger B\rangle$. Bottom right: $B$-sector bond observable represented by $\langle B^\dagger B\rangle$. The data support an $ABC$ ICDW structure, together with an $A$-sector incommensurate bond-order response.}
  \label{fig:phase2}
\end{figure*}

Because the same $A$ sector also carries modulated pairing structure in the project interpretation, we summarize this phase as
\begin{equation}
\text{Phase II: } ABC~\mathrm{ICDW},\qquad A~\mathrm{IBOW}+\mathrm{IPDW}.
\end{equation}
The evidence in Fig.~\ref{fig:phase2} directly supports the ICDW and IBOW parts of this statement.

\subsection{Phase III: intermediate phase at $U=0.6$}

The phase at $U=0.6$ is the most mixed regime in the present dataset. Figure~\ref{fig:phase3} displays the correlation function, entropy profile, orbital occupations, and the two bond observables. The density plot shows that the $A$ and $C$ sublattices are already close to a charge-ordered pattern, while the $B$ sector remains more weakly locked and still supports a correlation pattern compatible with residual topological character. For this reason we interpret phase III as a coexistence regime in which $AC$ are effectively CDW ordered, whereas the $B$ orbital still carries topological, bond-ordered, and pair-density-wave-like signatures.

\begin{figure*}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase3_U0p6_corr.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase3_U0p6_EE.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase3_U0p6_ni.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase3_U0p6_A_bond.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase3_U0p6_B_bond.png}
  \caption{Phase III (intermediate phase) represented by $U=0.6$. Top row: Majorana-related correlation function, entanglement entropy, and orbital-resolved density profile. Bottom row: $A$-sector and $B$-sector bond observables. The data support an interpretation in which the $A$ and $C$ orbitals are predominantly CDW ordered while the $B$ orbital retains topological and modulated bond/pair structure.}
  \label{fig:phase3}
\end{figure*}

We summarize the phase as
\begin{equation}
\text{Phase III: } AC~\mathrm{CDW},\qquad B~\mathrm{topological}+\mathrm{BOW}+\mathrm{PDW}.
\end{equation}
This is the central intermediate regime separating the two incommensurate windows.

\subsection{Phase IV: second ICDW phase at $U=1.5$}

At $U=1.5$ the spatial structure changes again. Figure~\ref{fig:phase4} shows the entropy, orbital-resolved density profile, and the two bond observables. The $A$ and $C$ sublattices remain close to a charge-ordered background, but the $B$ sector no longer resembles the weak-coupling topological regime. Instead it shows renewed incommensurate modulation, so we classify this state as a second ICDW regime in which $AC$ are effectively CDW ordered while the $B$ orbital carries ICDW and PDW-like structure.

\begin{figure*}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase4_U1p5_EE.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase4_U1p5_ni.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase4_U1p5_A_bond.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase4_U1p5_B_bond.png}
  \caption{Phase IV (second ICDW phase) represented by $U=1.5$. Top left: entanglement entropy. Top right: orbital-resolved occupation profile. Bottom left and right: bond observables in the $A$ and $B$ sectors. The numerical patterns support a state with $AC$ charge order and renewed incommensurate structure on the $B$ orbital.}
  \label{fig:phase4}
\end{figure*}

Our phase label is therefore
\begin{equation}
\text{Phase IV: } AC~\mathrm{CDW},\qquad B~\mathrm{ICDW}+\mathrm{PDW}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Phase V: strong-coupling CDW phase at $U=5$}

Finally, at strong coupling the real-space order is dominated by density locking. Figure~\ref{fig:phase5} shows the orbital-resolved density profile together with the $A$- and $B$-sector bond observables at $U=5$. All three sublattices exhibit strongly ordered profiles, so the global charge pattern is best described as an $ABC$ CDW state. At the same time, the $A$ sector still shows an identifiable bond modulation, which we interpret as a residual BOW component, and in the project language this is accompanied by PDW structure.

\begin{figure*}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase5_U5_ni.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase5_U5_A_bond.png}
  \includegraphics[width=0.32\textwidth]{figures/phases/phase5_U5_B_bond.png}
  \caption{Phase V (strong-coupling CDW phase) represented by $U=5$. Left: orbital-resolved occupation profile. Middle and right: bond observables on the $A$ and $B$ sectors. The system is globally in an $ABC$ CDW state, while the $A$ sector retains additional bond modulation.}
  \label{fig:phase5}
\end{figure*}

We therefore summarize the final regime as
\begin{equation}
\text{Phase V: } ABC~\mathrm{CDW},\qquad A~\mathrm{BOW}+\mathrm{PDW}.
\end{equation}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The main result of the revised analysis is that the interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain should be described by a five-phase structure rather than by a single weak-to-strong-coupling crossover. The advantage of this organization is that it reads the phase diagram directly from representative real-space observables. The phase labels are not assigned from one scalar order parameter alone; they arise from the combined evidence of entanglement, orbital occupations, bond structure, and Majorana-related correlations.

The physical sequence is also highly nontrivial. The first phase is topological, as expected from the noninteracting parent problem. Repulsion then drives the system into an incommensurate regime involving all three orbitals. The next step is not an immediate trivial CDW, but an intermediate phase in which different orbitals realize different orders simultaneously: the $A$ and $C$ sectors are predominantly charge ordered, while the $B$ sector still carries topological and modulated superconducting structure. Only after passing through a second incommensurate window does the system settle into a strong-coupling CDW phase.

This orbital differentiation is the most important conceptual point. The data indicate that the interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain cannot be understood as a one-component order parameter problem. The $A$, $B$, and $C$ sublattices respond differently to the same repulsive interaction, and this is what generates the observed coexistence of topological, ICDW, CDW, BOW, and PDW-like features in different windows of $U$.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Using phase-resolved real-space observables, we have reorganized the present DMRG dataset for the interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain into a five-phase picture:
\begin{align}
\text{I} &:\ \text{topological}, \nonumber\\
\text{II} &:\ ABC~\mathrm{ICDW},\ A~\mathrm{IBOW}+\mathrm{IPDW}, \nonumber\\
\text{III} &:\ AC~\mathrm{CDW},\ B~\mathrm{topological}+\mathrm{BOW}+\mathrm{PDW}, \nonumber\\
\text{IV} &:\ AC~\mathrm{CDW},\ B~\mathrm{ICDW}+\mathrm{PDW}, \nonumber\\
\text{V} &:\ ABC~\mathrm{CDW},\ A~\mathrm{BOW}+\mathrm{PDW}.
\end{align}
This is the structure now implemented in the manuscript and in the updated `work` repository. The immediate next step is to compile the revised RevTeX source and then refine the terminology of IBOW/IPDW/PDW with additional dedicated pairing plots if needed.
