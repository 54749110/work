\title{Interaction-driven reconstruction of geometric and topological correlations in a flat-band Lieb-Kitaev chain}

\author{Jiale Xuan}
\affiliation{School of Physics and Astronomy, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai 200240, China}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We study an interacting spinless Lieb-Kitaev chain in the nearly flat-band regime, motivated by the recent observation that Majorana zero modes in this system inherit two distinct spatial scales: an ordinary Bogoliubov-de Gennes localization length and a longer geometric length controlled by the quantum metric of the parent band. Using finite-system density-matrix renormalization group calculations together with a projected low-energy interpretation, we analyze how these structures evolve under nearest-neighbor density interaction. The present manuscript is intentionally written around the numerics that are already available, including orbital-resolved occupations, energy derivatives, entanglement-based central-charge estimates, bond observables, and extracted correlation lengths. The most robust trend is an interaction-driven orbital redistribution in which the central orbital approaches half filling while the side orbitals become increasingly imbalanced. This orbital reconstruction correlates with multiple anomalies in $dE/dU$, with enhanced bond and density correlations, and with a broad intermediate regime that is not captured by a single direct transition from a weak-coupling topological superconductor to a strong-coupling charge-density-wave state. At weak coupling the data remain compatible with a surviving long geometric Majorana scale, whereas at larger repulsion the system develops strong correlation effects, possible critical windows, and finally a conventional density-ordered regime. These results support the view that in flat-band topological superconductors quantum geometry and interaction-driven ordering are deeply intertwined.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Flat-band superconductivity has become a central theme in modern condensed-matter physics because the suppression of bare kinetic energy elevates the role of quantum geometry in setting collective transport and coherence scales~\cite{peotta2015,julku2016,torma2022}. In conventional single-band BCS systems, the coherence length is primarily controlled by dispersion and pairing amplitude. In contrast, multiband flat-band systems admit an additional geometric contribution: the quantum metric of Bloch wave functions can enter superfluid transport, pairing response, and real-space localization properties. The Lieb family of lattices is a canonical setting for this physics because it naturally supports nearly flat or exactly flat bands while retaining nontrivial orbital structure.

This geometric viewpoint became especially sharp in the recent analysis of Majorana zero modes in the noninteracting Lieb-Kitaev model~\cite{chen2024,guo2025}. There, the edge mode does not exhibit a single localization length. Instead, one finds two distinct correlation lengths: a short Bogoliubov-de Gennes scale associated with ordinary topological superconducting localization, and a longer geometric scale inherited from the spread of flat-band wave functions. The latter is controlled by the quantum metric and can become parametrically large in the nearly flat-band regime. This immediately raises a nontrivial many-body question: once interactions are introduced, does the geometric long tail survive as a physically meaningful scale, or is it washed out by correlation-driven reconstruction?

That question is important because interactions are unavoidable precisely in the regimes where flat-band geometry matters most. Reduced bandwidth amplifies the effect of density interactions, enhances the competition between superconductivity and charge ordering, and can generate phases that have no simple noninteracting counterpart. In one-dimensional topological superconductors, repulsive interactions are already known to reshape phase boundaries and to compete with pairing through commensurate or incommensurate density correlations~\cite{katsura2015,gangadharaiah2011,mahyaeh2020}. In a multiorbital flat-band system, the same competition should be even richer because interactions act on orbitally structured Wannier states rather than on a featureless single channel.

This broader motivation is reinforced by two complementary lines of reasoning developed in our project notes~\cite{flatbandnotes}. On the attractive side, a negative-$U$ interaction can in principle induce superconductivity even without imposing an explicit pairing field $\Delta$, making the flat-band Lieb geometry an intrinsic platform for interaction-generated superconducting order. On the repulsive side, a positive-$U$ interaction in the presence of finite $\Delta$ is expected to produce a nontrivial phase diagram rather than a trivial suppression of superconductivity: topological pairing, orbital redistribution, bond ordering, and density ordering can all compete on comparable footing. The present manuscript uses this physical picture to organize the available DMRG data into a phase-resolved PRB narrative.

\section{Model and theoretical framework}
\label{sec:model}

We consider a one-dimensional chain of unit cells $j=1,\dots,L$, each containing three spinless orbitals $(A_j,B_j,C_j)$ with annihilation operators $(a_j,b_j,c_j)$. Guided by the noninteracting Lieb-Kitaev construction~\cite{guo2025}, we write
\begin{equation}
H = H_t + H_\Delta + H_V + H_U,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
H_t &= \sum_j \Big[
t_1 a_j^\dagger b_j + t_1 c_j^\dagger b_j
+ t_2 a_{j+1}^\dagger b_j + t_2 c_j^\dagger b_{j+1}
+ \text{H.c.}
\Big], \\
H_\Delta &= \sum_j \Big[
\Delta_1 a_j b_j + \Delta_1 c_j b_j
+ \Delta_2 a_{j+1} b_j + \Delta_2 c_j b_{j+1}
+ \text{H.c.}
\Big], \\
H_V &= V \sum_j (\hat n_{C,j} - \hat n_{A,j})
- \mu \sum_j (\hat n_{A,j}+\hat n_{B,j}+\hat n_{C,j}), \\
H_U &= U \sum_j \left(\hat n_j-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\hat n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right).
\end{align}
Here $\hat n_{\alpha,j}$ is the number operator on orbital $\alpha\in\{A,B,C\}$, and $\hat n_j$ denotes the density combination used in the numerical implementation. The representative parameter set in the present dataset is
\begin{equation}
t=0.03,\qquad J=1,\qquad V=0.6,\qquad \Delta=0.018,\qquad \mu=0.
\end{equation}

For the noninteracting parent model, the cell-periodic Bloch states $\lvert u_n(k)\rangle$ define the quantum geometric tensor
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Q}_{\mu\nu}(k)
=
\langle \partial_\mu u(k)\vert
\left[1-\lvert u(k)\rangle\langle u(k)\rvert\right]
\vert \partial_\nu u(k)\rangle,
\end{equation}
whose real part gives the quantum metric~\cite{resta2011}
\begin{equation}
g_{\mu\nu}(k)=\mathrm{Re}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\mu\nu}(k).
\end{equation}
In one dimension, the corresponding geometric length is estimated schematically by
\begin{equation}
\xi_{\rm QM}
=
\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\rm BZ} dk\, g_{kk}(k)\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Recent work showed that the Majorana edge profile in the Lieb-Kitaev model inherits both a short localization length and a longer scale tied to $\xi_{\rm QM}$~\cite{chen2024,guo2025}. The central issue here is whether an interacting descendant of this long scale remains visible in many-body observables.

To interpret the intermediate-coupling regime we also use a projected low-energy viewpoint. After projection onto the isolated low-energy band, the effective Hamiltonian has the schematic form
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm proj}
=
\sum_k \varepsilon_k \gamma_k^\dagger \gamma_k
+ \sum_k \left(\Delta_k \gamma_k^\dagger \gamma_{-k}^\dagger + \text{H.c.}\right)
+ H_{\rm proj}^{(4)},
\end{equation}
where $H_{\rm proj}^{(4)}$ contains interaction-generated four-fermion terms dressed by multiorbital form factors. Bosonization of the resulting one-dimensional theory suggests the coexistence of pairing, commensurate density locking, and incommensurate density modulation,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}
=
\frac{v}{2\pi}\int dx \left[K(\partial_x\theta)^2 + K^{-1}(\partial_x\phi)^2\right]
- g_\Delta \int dx\,\cos(2\theta)
+ g_u \int dx\,\cos(4\phi)
+ g_q \int dx\,\cos(2\phi-Qx),
\end{equation}
which provides a natural phenomenology for multiple competing regimes~\cite{giamarchi2004,shankar2017,vondelft1998}.

\section{Numerical strategy}
\label{sec:numerics}

The interacting ground state is computed by finite-system DMRG with open boundary conditions using ITensor~\cite{fishman2022}. The three-orbital chain is mapped to a one-dimensional matrix-product-state sequence
\begin{equation}
(A_1,B_1,C_1,A_2,B_2,C_2,\dots,A_L,B_L,C_L).
\end{equation}
The source calculations emphasize careful sweep scheduling and convergence control, which is essential because the entanglement rises substantially in parts of the repulsive regime and because several observables show narrow anomalies as $U$ varies.

The main observables used below are entanglement entropy profiles, orbital-resolved occupations, Majorana-related correlation functions, and real-space bond observables. In the figure labels, the $A$-sector bond diagnostic is represented by the real-space pattern of the correlator stored in the \texttt{AdagA} dataset, while the $B$-sector diagnostic is represented by the \texttt{BdagB} dataset. When the entropy profile is compatible with conformal scaling, the central charge can be estimated from the open-chain formula
\begin{equation}
S(\ell)=\frac{c}{6}\ln\left[\frac{2L}{\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi \ell}{L}\right)\right]+s_0.
\end{equation}
In the present phase-resolved discussion, however, the real-space morphology of the raw observables is more informative than any single fitted scalar quantity.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The global scan of the repulsive branch remains important, especially the bulk-averaged occupations as functions of $U$, which show the strongest evidence for interaction-driven orbital redistribution. Within that broader picture, the phase-resolved data are most clearly discussed through five representative regimes. We therefore retain the broader interpretation of the manuscript while using the following five subsections to present the decisive real-space evidence phase by phase.

\subsection{Phase I: topological phase}

Phase I is the weak-coupling topological regime. Figure~\ref{fig:phase1} collects the Majorana-related correlation function, the entanglement entropy profile, and the orbital-resolved density profile at $U=0$. The correlation function exhibits the smooth long-ranged structure expected for the topological parent state, while the entropy profile remains consistent with a gapped regime. The sublattice occupations are already unequal, but they do not yet show the pronounced interaction-driven incommensurate locking that appears at larger $U$.

We therefore identify phase I as the topological reference state from which the interaction-driven reconstruction begins.

\subsection{Phase II: ICDW phase at $U=0.15$}

The second regime is represented by $U=0.15$. Figure~\ref{fig:phase2} shows the entanglement entropy, the full $ABC$ occupation profile, the single-$B$ occupation profile, and the two bond diagnostics. The density profile now displays clear incommensurate modulation on all three sublattices, which motivates the classification of the whole state as an $ABC$ incommensurate charge-density-wave phase. The separate $B$-orbital profile is included because it shows directly that the central orbital participates in this modulation rather than remaining a passive background channel.

The bond observables further sharpen the interpretation. The $A$-sector \texttt{AdagA} pattern develops a pronounced oscillatory structure, supporting the assignment of an incommensurate bond-order response on the $A$ orbital. The available $B$-sector \texttt{BdagB} profile is taken from the nearest available dataset to $U=0.15$ and is included only as a supporting indicator of the central-channel modulation. In the language of the project, this phase is summarized as $ABC$ ICDW with additional $A$-sector IBOW and IPDW character.

\subsection{Phase III: intermediate phase at $U=0.6$}

The phase at $U=0.6$ is the intermediate regime where different sublattices support different kinds of order simultaneously. Figure~\ref{fig:phase3} shows the Majorana-related correlation function, entanglement entropy, orbital occupations, and the \texttt{AdagA}/\texttt{BdagB} bond diagnostics. The occupation profile indicates that the $A$ and $C$ sectors are already close to a charge-ordered pattern, whereas the $B$ orbital remains the least trivial channel. At the same time, the correlation function still retains a nontrivial topological-like structure, which supports the interpretation that the $B$ sector continues to carry the remnant topological response.

The bond data are consistent with this mixed character. In the phase language used throughout the project, the most economical interpretation is
\begin{equation}
\text{Phase III: } AC~\mathrm{CDW},\qquad B~\mathrm{topological}+\mathrm{BOW}+\mathrm{PDW}.
\end{equation}
This is the central coexistence regime separating the two incommensurate windows.

\subsection{Phase IV: second ICDW phase at $U=1.5$}

At $U=1.5$ the system enters a second incommensurate regime. Figure~\ref{fig:phase4} shows the entropy profile, the full $ABC$ occupations, the separate $B$-orbital occupation profile, and the two bond diagnostics. Relative to phase III, the $B$ sector now exhibits renewed incommensurate structure rather than the residual topological character emphasized in the intermediate regime. Meanwhile the $A$ and $C$ orbitals remain close to a charge-ordered background.

This supports the phase assignment
\begin{equation}
\text{Phase IV: } AC~\mathrm{CDW},\qquad B~\mathrm{ICDW}+\mathrm{PDW}.
\end{equation}
The separate $B$-profile panel is especially useful here because it makes the incommensurate modulation of the central channel explicit.

\subsection{Phase V: strong-coupling CDW phase at $U=5$}

Finally, at strong coupling the real-space order is dominated by density locking. Figure~\ref{fig:phase5} shows the orbital-resolved occupations together with the \texttt{AdagA} and \texttt{BdagB} diagnostics at $U=5$. All three sublattices display strong density modulation, which identifies the global state as an $ABC$ CDW phase. At the same time, the $A$ channel retains a pronounced bond pattern, consistent with the project interpretation that the strong-coupling regime still contains residual $A$-sector BOW and PDW structure.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The present data support three claims with high confidence. First, the bulk-averaged occupations show a strong and systematic orbital redistribution under repulsion, with the central orbital moving toward half filling and therefore emerging as the dominant low-energy channel. Second, the combination of phase-resolved entanglement, density, correlation, and bond data strongly disfavors a single direct transition between weak-coupling topology and strong-coupling CDW order. Third, the weak-coupling regime remains qualitatively consistent with the two-length-scale Majorana picture inherited from the noninteracting flat-band model, even though the interpretation becomes more intricate once intermediate-coupling density reconstruction sets in.

The five-phase organization is therefore useful not because it replaces the broader scan, but because it gives that scan a physically resolved interpretation. Phase I is topological. Phase II is an $ABC$ ICDW regime with additional $A$-sector IBOW and IPDW signatures. Phase III is a mixed state in which $AC$ behave as a charge-ordered background while the $B$ channel remains topological and simultaneously supports bond-order and PDW-like structure. Phase IV is a second incommensurate window in which the $B$ channel is better described as ICDW plus PDW. Phase V is the strong-coupling $ABC$ CDW state with residual $A$-sector bond structure.

Several aspects remain provisional. Some phase labels, especially those involving PDW/IPDW language, are inferred from the broader project interpretation rather than from a dedicated pairing correlator included in the present figure set. Likewise, one panel in phase II uses the nearest available $B$-sector bond dataset rather than an exactly matching $U=0.15$ output. Those caveats do not change the main conclusion: the interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain is best understood as an orbitally differentiated flat-band system in which topology, incommensurability, and charge ordering are all reorganized by repulsion.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have reorganized the current DMRG results for the interacting Lieb-Kitaev chain into a PRB-style manuscript that combines a broad interaction-scan perspective with a five-phase real-space interpretation. The most robust global result remains the interaction-driven redistribution of the orbital occupations, particularly the tendency of the central $B$ orbital toward half filling as $U$ increases. The phase-resolved figures then show how this redistribution is expressed locally through topological correlations, ICDW structure, mixed intermediate order, and eventual strong-coupling CDW locking.

The resulting physical picture is not a simple direct collapse from topological superconductivity to a trivial density-ordered state. Instead, the repulsive branch with finite pairing supports a sequence of distinct regimes in which the $A$, $B$, and $C$ orbitals play different roles. This is the central message of the revised manuscript and the organizing principle for the updated \texttt{work} project.
